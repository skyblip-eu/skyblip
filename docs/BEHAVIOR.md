## core - the logic the radio and the screen are built on

### test/core/test_adsl.cpp

ADS-L position packet: the safety-critical round-trip and error-correction tests: a radio protocol tested with zero RF hardware. The RF channel is reduced to an injectable BER; we distinguish *detected failure* from *silent miscorrection* (the real anti-collision danger).

**adsl**

- Monte-Carlo BER — detected vs silent miscorrection accounting
- an over-range altitude encodes the limit, not 'unavailable'
- an over-range ground speed encodes the limit, not 'unavailable'
- an over-range sink rate encodes the limit, not 'unavailable'
- field accessors round-trip through the wire layout
- from_own marks what own-ship does not know
- multi-bit errors within flagged (weak) bits are corrected
- scramble+crc, then descramble+check round-trip is lossless (BER 0)
- single-bit error is corrected via CRC syndrome
- the invalid codes are reachable on purpose and round-trip

### test/core/test_air.cpp

The shared sync window: one M-band dwell, both systems. If any of these fail, the receiver is deaf to half the sky and does not know it.

**air**

- a chip error inside the frame is reported, not hidden
- a foreign sync word frames as nothing
- a report shorter than the dwell reads is refused
- an ADS-L burst frames as ADS-L, byte-aligned on its data
- an ALP-TAS burst frames as ALP-TAS through the same window
- one flipped chip in the sync tail still names the system
  > A dead chip pair in the sync tail must not cost the frame: it is 23 bits long and the two systems differ in far more than one of them.
- the constexpr chip coding is the one fec::manchester_encode uses
- the two sync words share the window the detector matches
  > The trick: the two sync words are different, their chips agree for 18, and the 16 the detector is armed with are the byte-aligned middle of that agreement.

### test/core/test_alptas.cpp

ALP-TAS (FLARM-wire, 2024 protocol) air-frame codec. Two things are worth testing here and both are safety-critical: that a frame we transmit is the frame the protocol says (encode -> decode round trip, at several latitudes and across the 20-bit position wrap), and that a frame that is NOT ours is thrown away (frame CRC, plus the decrypt-plausibility gate that is the only thing standing between a mis-framed foreign packet and the traffic table).

**alptas**

- a non-position message type is not decoded as traffic
- aircraft type maps back to the ADS-L category it came from
- altitude round trips including below sea level and high up
- an ICAO address keeps its table, an unknown one degrades to 0
- encode then decode preserves the identity and motion fields
- encoding refuses to claim a position it does not have
- on-ground and airborne flight state survive the 2-bit field
- position round trips across the 20-bit coding wrap
- position round trips north, south, high latitude and near zero
- speed, climb and track round trip over their ranges
- the decrypt gate accepts the second it was sent in, +/-1
- the decrypt gate rejects a frame with one byte changed
- the decrypt gate rejects random frames
- the frame CRC is set, checked, and a flipped bit fails it

### test/core/test_atmosphere.cpp

**atmosphere**

### test/core/test_comms.cpp

### test/core/test_timing.cpp

**timing**

- PPS lost within holdover — still receiving, no slotted TX
- TX only in M-band direct slots when clock is anchored
- a dwell stops early enough to retune before the next one
- band and channel per dwell
- dwell map matches the decided band split
  > Our cut of the second: two 400 ms M-band dwells, one channel each, so slot 1 runs 800..1200 and its tail is the head of the next second. The uplink dwell is framed on the window our own ground station transmits in (210..390 ms, a burst that completes by 390). The M-band dwell opens at 400 to catch FLARM-generation traffic that starts around 405, earlier than the ADS-L direct slot our own bursts respect.
- every band edge keeps its guard
- past holdover or no UTC — listen only, fail closed
- uplink window and direct slot predicates

**transmit**

- a busy band forces a transmission, then goes quiet
  > §D.3: force after 3000 ms of failed attempts, then 2000 ms off air.
- a stale fix is not transmitted
  > §G.1.16: the navigation solution must not be older than 500 ms.
- consecutive transmissions alternate channel and slot
- nothing goes out unless the slot allows it
- one burst per second airborne, one per ten on the ground
  > §G.1.16: at least 1 Hz airborne, 0.1 Hz on the ground.
- the burst completes inside the direct slot, tail or no tail
  > §C.2.5: traffic alternates between the two M-band channels. The second dwell hears traffic past 1000 ms; the direct slot ends there, so our own burst never does.
- the first instant of a dwell is the moment it opens
  > The upper channel's dwell opens at 800 already tuned - the hop guard before it is what paid for the retune - so 800 is its first legal instant.
- the instant is inside the direct slot, with room for the burst
- two devices do not pick the same instant every second

**epd**

- a present after deep sleep wakes the panel with a reset pulse
- begin() runs the SSD1681 init sequence and resets the panel

- present() is non-blocking and ready() settles the panel into deep sleep
- present() rewrites the previous-image bank so the panel diffs the truth

### test/products/test_rf_timing.cpp

What is on the 868 MHz air, and when. Every burst in these tests is a real scrambled ADS-L frame put on the channel at an absolute instant; the radio hears it only if the firmware had the receiver tuned there at that moment. A slot-map regression shows up as DEAF records, not as a missing feature.

**rf**

- a burst is heard only inside the dwell that owns its channel
- a burst that straddles the second is dated by when it started
  > A burst near the end of the second finishes after the second has rolled over. Its phase is its own instant's, not the instant we noticed it ended.
- a heard burst is the frame that was on air, decoded by the real path
- listen before talk holds a burst on a busy channel
  > §D.3: the executor samples the carrier and holds the burst while the channel is busy. The dwell's end is what gives up, and it says so.
- on the ground the transmit rate drops to 0.1 Hz
- own-ship transmits once a second, inside its window, alternating channel
- what own-ship put on air decodes back to own-ship state
- without an anchored clock nothing is transmitted

### test/products/test_scenarios.cpp

**scenario**

- the parser reads ownship, traffic and events

The policy under test: refresh only when the pixels changed, fast (flicker-free) by default, and schedule the flashing full refresh around the traffic picture — never during an alarm, opportunistically once the sky has been quiet, forced only at the hard ceiling.

**screen policy**

- presents wait for the panel, none is issued mid-refresh
- the hard ceiling forces the full even mid-alarm
